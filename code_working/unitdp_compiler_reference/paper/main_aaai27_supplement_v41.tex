\begin{abstract}
This document defines the complete registration core and its validation
judgment, quantifies strict contract-only versus full-lifecycle rejection,
reconstructs the 378/42/0 handler-splice counterfactual, and gives a controlled
48-substitution P/F matched-shell test.  It then details bounded conservative
extension, a four-handler external-accountant stress test with 756 sealed
hybrid rejections, and the 2,286-contract lattice plus accountant, execution,
and artifact evidence for three owner-level differentially private stochastic
gradient descent (DP-SGD) mechanisms.  It also gives the
closest-work ownership boundary and separates source/report verification from
checks requiring raw data or private research randomizers.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and Reading Guide}

The main contribution is a finite pre-execution registration method, not any
of the three route mechanisms.  P is Bernoulli/add-remove, F is
fixed-size/replace-one with simple random sampling without replacement
(SRSWOR), and A is prior random allocation/Balanced Iteration Subsampling
(BIS) under add/remove.  H is a separately registered privacy-loss distribution
(PLD) accountant overlay that retains A's mechanism and executor.
Here and below, DP abbreviates differential privacy.
This supplement first fixes their execution shell, then gives the
contract-only lifecycle attribution, complete-core registration object,
simpler handler-boundary ablation, matched-shell route control, exact old-route
preservation, and contract lattice.  Later sections document closest-work
ownership, route-specific accountant/executor checks, data profiles, evidence
identifiers, and reproduction.

Every reported execution is explicitly a non-release research run.  Raw
third-party data, complete private traces, owner assignments, and the five
fixed research randomizers are not redistributed.  Section~\ref{sec:reproduce}
separates source/report checks from fresh raw-data executions.
Here a ``separately implemented'' oracle or reference means code that does not
import the corresponding production implementation; it does not mean an
external team or independent human reproduction.

\section{Route Semantics and Conditional Scope}
\label{sec:routes}

\paragraph{Common owner update.}
Let $D_u$ contain all records attributed to owner $u$.  Preparation requires
each retained record to have one owner.  A fixed owner-local policy retains at
most $K$ records and selects at most $L$ records when $u$ participates.  The
mean-loss gradient is clipped once:
\[
 \bar g_{u,s}=g_{u,s}
 \min\!\left\{1,\frac{C}{\lVert g_{u,s}\rVert_2}\right\}.
\]
Here $\theta_s$ is the model state, $B_s$ the participating-owner set, $\eta$
the learning rate, $C$ the clipping threshold, $\sigma$ the noise multiplier,
and $I$ the identity matrix.
Every route applies
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \theta_{s+1}&=\theta_s-\frac{\eta}{D}
 \left(\sum_{u\in B_s}\bar g_{u,s}+Z_s\right),\\
 Z_s&\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2C^2I),
\end{aligned}
\]
where $D$ is public and never the realized participation count.

\paragraph{P and F.}
P fixes $q,T,C,\sigma,D$ and independently includes each present owner at
each step; an empty sample still produces noise and an optimizer update.  F
fixes $N,m,T,C,\sigma,D=m$ and uses SRSWOR to draw exactly $m$ distinct public
positions.
Replacing one position changes the clipped sum by at most $2C$, hence
normalized accountant noise $\sigma/2$ \citep{wang2019subsampled}.

\paragraph{A.}
At each of $E$ epochs every owner independently samples a uniform subset of
exactly $k$ distinct positions among $t$ candidate steps, giving total schedule
length $T=Et$.  An owner participates
exactly there, so participation is coupled across steps.  The number active at
one step is random, and empty steps remain noisy.  The plan fixes
$t,k,E,C,\sigma,D$; assignments and realized participation remain private.
This is the prior random-allocation/BIS mechanism
\citep{shenfeld2025randomallocation,dong2026lessrandom}.

\paragraph{Conditional privacy scope.}
Assume a release procedure is total over its declared adjacent domain,
performs the fixed schedule without a data-dependent abort, keeps validation
outputs internal, fixes preprocessing and the owner-local policy independently
of protected input, contributes at most one norm-$C$ vector per participating
owner, and adds independent Gaussian noise at every step.  P then invokes its
Poisson-Gaussian analysis; F invokes its fixed-size analysis at sensitivity
$2C$; A invokes its bidirectional random-allocation bound.  The applicable
prior analysis and adaptive composition give the registered
$(\varepsilon,\delta)$ guarantee \citep{mironov2017renyi,
shenfeld2025randomallocation}.  Division by fixed $D$, optimization, and model
release are post-processing.

This restates prior premises.  Registration does not prove that an executor
satisfies them.  Exact public-fixture compilers can reject changed inputs and
are not total release procedures.  Deployment additionally needs secure
randomness, safe failure behavior, composition, and operational review.

\section{Complete-Core Registration Judgment}

A handler $h$ stores route/schema identity, nominal privacy tuple, exact
contract and compiled types, parser, compiler, route-local registry $r_h$, exception
types, integrity method, evidence-report binding, and witness map $W$.  Its
canonical core excludes only the evidence-report path and digest, avoiding a
circular commitment:
\[
 K(h)=\mathsf{canon}(\mathit{id},\mathit{schema},\rho,
 \tau_c,\tau_P,p,c,r_h,W).
\]

\paragraph{Route-local evidence seal.}
The evidence report contains status \texttt{PASS}, route/schema identity, the
full serialized $K(h)$, its canonical digest, all seven obligations, and an
upstream route-evidence digest.  Registration checks the report file digest,
report-payload digest, embedded core digest, live core digest, exact
route/schema, nominal registry fields, executor status, unique obligation set,
callable resolution, and every source digest.  Writing
$\mathsf{SHA256}(\cdot)$ for the SHA-256 digest, the central premise is
\[
 \mathsf{SHA256}(e.K)=e.K_{\rm hash}=\mathsf{SHA256}(K(h)).
\]
The report path and file digest are bound separately by $h$.  A consistently
wrong core can still pass; this is a binding predicate, not a privacy or
program proof.

\paragraph{Generic dispatch.}
The immutable registry maps exact route ids to validated handlers.  Parsing
performs exact lookup, revalidates the handler, checks schema, invokes its
parser, and checks the exact returned type and nominal identity.  Compilation
revalidates, invokes its compiler, checks the exact plan type and unchanged
contract, then calls the registered integrity method.  Static inspection finds
no P/F/A route-id constant, contract type, or \texttt{if route\_id ==} branch
in these generic parser/compiler functions.

Route-local compilation still checks the exact benchmark profile,
mapping/attribution, owner-local policy, fixed preprocessor, accountant, and
executor/source binding.  Execution rechecks the compiled object before and
after the loop and links the model, execution, bundle, summary, and collection.
These later boundaries do not substitute for registration because they are
selected by the handler that registration admits.

\paragraph{Bounded conservative extension.}
Let $R'=R\uplus\{j\mapsto h_j\}$ with a fresh exact key and passing
registration.  If an old handler, its sources, and compilation inputs are
unchanged, exact lookup selects the same immutable handler, so parsing and
compilation are unchanged.  This is handler-local and syntactic, not semantic
correctness of $h_j$.  With $R=\{P,F\}$ and $h_j=A$, all six old contracts and
public plans equal their before-extension and frozen predecessor objects,
including exact canonical digests.  All nine P/F/A profile contracts compile.
Eight malformed registrations---missing/duplicate obligation, wrong source or
report digest, parser/type splice, unvalidated executor, and duplicate
route---fail.

\section{Route-A Accountant and Execution Evidence}

\paragraph{Registered conservative calculation.}
Let $b=\lfloor t/k\rfloor$ and $r=kE$ for $E$ epochs.  The implementation
applies the registered direct random-allocation reduction: remove-direction
R\'enyi differential privacy (RDP) is
\[
 R_\alpha^{\rm rem}=r\,R_\alpha^{\rm alloc}(b,\sigma),
\]
evaluated at integer $\alpha=2,\ldots,16$ and converted at $\delta$.  The
add-direction upper bound uses
\[
 \sigma_{\rm eff}=\sigma\sqrt{b/r},\qquad
 \varepsilon_{\rm add}=
 \varepsilon_{\rm G}(\sigma_{\rm eff},\delta)
 +\frac{r(1-1/b)}{2\sigma^2}.
\]
The reported value is
$\max\{\varepsilon_{\rm rem},\varepsilon_{\rm add}\}$.  These formulas
instantiate prior theory; the contribution is their registration and
cross-binding, not their derivation.

\section{Validated Scope and Extension Boundary}

The current implementation instantiates three mechanism handlers and one
external-accountant overlay.  Within that declared surface, the 378 original
hybrids, 756 four-handler hybrids, and 2,286 contract nonendpoints give
exhaustive decisions over their prespecified binary partitions.  The 60-case P
lifecycle and 48 matched-shell P/F substitutions provide controlled stage and
obligation attribution.  H demonstrates integration of a separately
registered accountant while deliberately retaining A's mechanism and executor.
These are exact finite-universe results; new obligations, mechanisms, or
third-party plugins require corresponding handlers, analyses, and evidence.

The complete-core seal binds the seven declared obligations, compiled object,
report, and evidence.  Its trust boundary is the completeness and correctness
of those declarations and of the report builder.  SHA-256 commits the declared
bytes under collision resistance and an honest source.  Signed provenance,
independently reproduced builds, and runtime, randomness, hardware, or
side-channel attestation are complementary layers for stronger deployment
assurance.

The public fixtures, finite references, linear models, and five-run collections
establish compilation/execution correspondence at the reported scale.  The
isolated 111 MB H environment and measured 31--337 second accounting/compile
times characterize its integration cost.  The portable archive binds the exact
distributions, trees, requests, responses, and reports, while that numerical
environment remains hash-bound rather than redistributed or re-executed.
Larger models, broader input refinement, and comparative utility or scalability
studies are natural extensions of this validation surface.  Multi-owner
records, other adjacency relations, shuffled or structured sampling, and new
models likewise require route-specific analyses and registrations.  Deployment
additionally requires secure randomness and safe failure handling; private
diagnostics require a separate privacy analysis, and repeated model releases
require composition.
