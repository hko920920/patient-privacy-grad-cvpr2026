We thank the AC for summarizing concerns about practicality, comparisons, and clarity. We clarify the key operational distinction and address each axis.

**1. Practicality and the 48.6-s figure.** PP-Mark is deliberately dual-mode. Score is the routine screening path: per-image verification takes 0.72 s and tolerates lossy transformations. As submission reported, the 48.6-s cost is incurred once per origin artifact to generate a cacheable Accept receipt only for high-assurance uses such as legal evidence, regulatory audit, or untrusted-generator verification, not routine screening. JPEG or resizing does not trigger re-proving; only a derivative that itself requires cryptographic Accept needs a new receipt. SHA-256 exactness is therefore the intended Accept semantics, while Score supplies transformation tolerance. Score alone does not certify generator compliance; the Accept receipt does.

We added the requested cumulative-degradation test on the same 100 images, with no survivor filtering and a frozen threshold: JPEG-q25 detects 94/100, crop-resize 87/100, and persistent crop-resize-JPEG 80/100. We also measured the proof trade-off. N=200 reduces prove/verify time from 48.572/7.589 s to 32.309/4.468 s. PLONK and Groth16 shrink the 40.68-MB SP1 artifact to 3,874 B and 1,499 B, but increase proving to 312.677 s and 438.380 s. SP1 is the fastest validated full-size proof tested; smaller artifacts required slower proving.

For re-encoding, Score obtains the binding and model/inversion declaration from the artifact, not pixels.

**2. Quantitative and engineering comparisons.** Cross-method experiments were present but dispersed across Tables 3-4 and Appendices F-K. At the common 1% clean FPR, baseline transfer FARs (TR/GS) span .0100-.0400/.0100-.0267, versus .0033/.0067 for Score and 0/0 for Accept; white-box FAR is 1.00 for every baseline, .76 for Score, and 0 for Accept. We will consolidate them.

The submission already reported PP-Mark quality (BRISQUE p95 34.14; KID 1.101 +/- .202 x10^-3). The omission was matched baseline quality. We supplied BRISQUE, KID, and CLIPScore for all methods: baseline ranges are 27.35-105.86, -.989 to 7.239 x10^-3, and 31.30-32.54, respectively, versus PP-Mark 34.14, 1.101 x10^-3, and 31.64. Unbiased finite-sample KID can be slightly negative. We do not claim best BRISQUE; this places measured quality beside forgery resistance.

We also implemented the requested Ed25519 controls. Bare-Sig signs the image hash; Context-Sig also signs context and public binding. Both verify 100/100 originals and reject 100/100 replays. Yet an authorized signer can validly sign 100/100 unwatermarked images, while both PP-Mark modes accepted 0/100 in the submitted compliance-bypass evaluation: signatures authenticate assertions but do not verify embedding. Both signatures also fail after JPEG or crop-resize, while submitted Score retains 94/100 and 87/100 detections. We narrow the claim accordingly: a standard signature is preferable for exact integrity alone; PP-Mark adds embedding-compliance verification and a transform-tolerant signal.

For SEAL, we clarified that semantic-conditioned detection and exact public receipt binding target different guarantees: exact semantic-hash equality remains brittle, while fuzzy matching creates a receipt-reuse neighborhood. This is not a flaw in SEAL, whose detector still checks each inverted latent. We report a behavioral transformation comparison with an explicit calibration caveat. DGS protects a managed decoder's gradient channel, whereas PP-Mark protects an offline public acceptance rule; they are complementary.

**3. Clarity and scope.** We will restore the notation table and revise Figure 1 to expose P1-P4, public/private inputs, and the Score/Accept branches. We withdraw "architecture-agnostic": SD2.1 and SDXL support only a claim across two latent-diffusion models, and Score requires a compatible latent embedding/recovery interface. The target is provenance for participating generators, not universal detection of arbitrary open-weight outputs.

The receipt has no user/account identifier, and bindings change with context. Identical contexts link to one context-key pair; deployment key identifiers reveal producer/key epoch and become user-linking only if assigned per account. We make this boundary explicit.
